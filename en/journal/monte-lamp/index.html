Print One Segment First, Then Design the Lamp

The first piece we printed became the rule for the rest.

This line of light turns a corner, and it is cut to the seats.

Straight You Can Buy. Curved You Cannot.

Acrylic diffusers exist off the shelf. Straight ones you can buy and cut to length. They simply will not bend. The bar at Monte is an arc, and there is no stock curved version. Force a bend and the section collapses, and the line stops being one line. So we printed it, and the straight runs of this lamp were printed too.

Accept the Limit First. Then Design.

The printer could not make a piece as long as 66 cm. That is the limit, and it was simply accepted. Only then did design begin: divide the length evenly, and a joint is only a joint, falling where no one is sitting.

What Stopped Us Was Colour

What really stopped us was not strength. It was colour. The samples came back from two suppliers, one resin whiter, one yellower, and a single wrong segment shows the moment the light comes on.

The First Segment Is the Standard

So we printed one segment first and measured it. That segment became the standard: size and colour fixed together, and the frame, the hanging points and the spacing designed backwards from it. The joints are not hidden. Each one wears a stainless steel ring, with a one-watt light set into the ring’s lower edge, one per seat, so the light on the counter stays even. Wherever a joint breaks the light, a light fills it in.

That Gap Is There on Purpose

The heat lamp behind is pulled up and down. Join it to the main light and every pull would shake a guest’s table. We left a 45 mm gap between them.

How it is segmented and joined, we keep to ourselves. What comes apart in the film is the original model of this lamp; the printer footage was recorded while we printed the first segment; the finished shots are all at Monte. Nine segments, and not one reprinted.

"Not design first, then print. Print first, then design."

If your restaurant, bar or counter needs a light that cannot be bought, bring the idea for your shop and the site, and we will run the numbers together first.
