How to Set a Restaurant Fit-Out Budget, and Where It Overruns

A budget is a list of things you have not decided yet.

A restaurant fit-out budget usually overruns somewhere the guest never sees. We went back through the estimates and variation records of several restaurants we have built, and most of the money that went over sat behind the walls, inside the ceiling, and in functions decided after the estimate.

So this piece does not give a price per ping. We quote commercial projects individually, because pricing by floor area becomes too distorted to be a useful reference, and restaurants more than most: equipment, extraction and the condition of the site pull the unit price apart. Instead, this is about what eats a budget and how to plan for it.

1. First, separate the pockets inside the budget

The number in a budget and the number on a quotation are often not the same scope. Our estimates group cost roughly like this: base works (demolition, temporary works, electrical and plumbing, masonry), fit-out works (joinery, paint, glass, lighting), equipment works (air-conditioning, kitchen, refrigeration, fire protection), then design fees, a supervision fee of 12% of construction cost, and finally 5% business tax. One more item is often left out: the architect's sign-off for fire safety and interior renovation permits, which is charged separately.

Settle one more thing before you sign: who buys the equipment. On a 110-ping restaurant we built, air-conditioning, kitchen and refrigeration were listed as paid by the owner. On a 30-ping bistro, air-conditioning and fire protection sat inside our estimate. Either way works, but the two budget numbers do not cover the same scope.

2. How big a share the unseen works take

In the estimate for a 30-ping bistro in an old building, temporary works, electrical and plumbing, and masonry together made up nearly half of the main works. The joinery, paint, glass, lighting and timber flooring a guest can see added up to a little over a third. 【待 Mo 確認：這兩個比例出自 Belvoir 的估價單，可否以匿名方式公開】

Nothing went wrong there. Old buildings and restaurants are simply like that: services are re-run, waterproofing is dealt with, extraction and drainage need a route. But if the budget is set by what can be seen, it starts well short.

3. How variations happen

Variations are not accidents. They are things the estimate did not mention, or that had not yet been decided, and which appeared later. We compared two settlements. They differ greatly in size, yet look alike in origin.

On a 14-ping wine bar, five items were varied at settlement: electrical and plumbing, air-conditioning, joinery, temporary works and metalwork, together about 9% of the original construction cost. Paint and special effects, the two largest lines, did not move. Temporary works had been written at estimate stage as provisional, charged at actual cost, and were settled on that basis. Every variation was marked as confirmed on the estimate.

On a 110-ping restaurant, three rounds of variations followed the initial quote, adding about half of the original quote, including equipment bought by the owner. 【待 Mo 確認：這個比例出自 IRON CHEF 的追加報價，可否以匿名方式公開，或整段刪掉】

These variations fall into three kinds. The first is site condition that only shows once you are there: sprinkler heads to add and relocate, a ceiling with nothing to hang from that needed strengthening, heavy oil contamination in the car parking bay to clear. The second is function decided after the estimate: automatic doors, switchable glass, a concealed door, the platform structure of a wine room, indirect lighting. The third is specification change: counter stone swapped to another range.

4. How to plan for it

First, write what is undecided into the estimate rather than leaving it blank. Provisionally allow a figure where you can and mark it as charged at actual cost; where the condition is unknown, say the item will be priced after it is checked on site. Demolition we never price from drawings. We look at the site first.

Second, confirm every variation. Items outside scope are quoted separately, as set out in our FAQ; at settlement, each variation is marked as confirmed on the estimate.

Third, fix equipment and dimensions early. The sooner kitchen, air-conditioning and extraction sizes are on the drawings, the fewer surprises on site. An accurate drawing makes an accurate quotation, because the quotation is built item by item from quantities on the drawings.

Fourth, keep a contingency, but not as a single percentage. The two examples above, around 9% and around half, differ by a multiple, and no single figure answers both. It is more practical to estimate by the three kinds above, one kind at a time. 【待 Mo 補：ON 建議業主預留的預備金做法或比例】

“A budget does not overrun suddenly. What was left blank at signing gets filled in, one item at a time, on site.”

Design fees, the supervision fee, and why commercial projects are quoted individually are in what design costs per ping. What else to prepare before work starts is in what a designer will ask you to prepare before you open a restaurant.
