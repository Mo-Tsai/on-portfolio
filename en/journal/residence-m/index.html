A Path, Not a Set of Rooms — Ceiling Height in Place of Partitions

The ceiling is a river; the space's rhythm flows with it.

The ceiling here is not a plane. It is a run of continuous curved surfaces that opens at the entry, passes through the living room and carries on over the kitchen island.

The rise and fall follow the use of the space: a little higher over the living room, pressed lower over the island. Height defines the zones, and no partition is needed. What a wall does, a ceiling can do too, and do more quietly.

Most Things Step Back. One Steps Forward.

The island, in deep blue lacquer with a stone top, is the heaviest stroke in the whole pale grey space. The colour is not a decorative accent. It is the centre of gravity: when most surfaces in a room are stepping back, one element has to step forward and say that this is the heart of the kitchen.

The natural stone wall at the entry runs from floor to ceiling, its veining surfacing under indirect light. A yellow armchair and an orange desk are two deliberate points of brightness. The space is mostly grey, so colour has to be used sparingly to be seen at all.

A Base That Changes with the Light

Mineral coating and a terrazzo floor form the base of the space. Both have a fine grain, and both read lighter or darker as the light changes: cooler by day, warmer at night. Choosing materials that change with the light gives the space two expressions in a single day, without adding a single shape.

"The whole home is a path, not a set of rooms."

Moving through it, you pass no door frame and feel no change of room. Only the height of the ceiling shifts, the light turns with it, and the floor changes quietly underfoot. Where a partition would once have divided, there is a single continuous curve.
