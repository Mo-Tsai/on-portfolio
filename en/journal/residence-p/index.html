A Collection Does Not Need a Gallery. It Needs a Daily Life.

A collection does not need a gallery — it needs a daily life.

The heart of this home is not the design. It is the pieces the owner has collected over the years: hand-carved wooden doors, an old screen, Persian rugs, potted plants. The design did not start from a style. It started from a question: how do objects that already carry stories keep speaking in a new space?

The New Steps Back. The Old Speaks.

The storage at the entry is built around an old hand-carved wooden door, and the new white frame steps back into the background so the grain and colour of the door lead the corridor. A Persian rug runs out underfoot, bringing the density of traditional craft into a modern plan. Where new meets old there is no special transition; they are simply placed together, because these pieces do not need dressing up.

The living room takes a screen as its focal point, with a blue fabric sofa and rosewood furniture side by side. The palette is not a formula for a “Chinese style”; it is a true reflection of how the owner lives. Every piece has a provenance, and the design’s job is to let them share a room without quarrelling.

Let the Gap in Time Exist

The sheen of the marble floor sits against the dull surface of the old timber. That contrast was not something the designer made; it is simply the time between the materials. New materials reflect light; old ones absorb it. Letting that gap exist, instead of trying to unify it, is the attitude of this home.

"The old pieces are no longer objects to be looked at. They are part of life. That is the best home a collection can have."

When a carved door becomes a cabinet front, a Persian rug becomes the floor you cross every day, and the plants go on growing on the balcony, the collection stops being displayed and starts being used. That is what we did here: not turning the owner’s taste into a style, but making the space to the way he lives.
