ON Stands for Two Words

Operation and Narrative. Not the preposition.

Most people read our name as the preposition — the on of a switch, of a light. Some say it is easy to remember. Others ask whether it comes from "on trend."

It is neither. ON is an abbreviation of two words: Operation and Narrative.

Operation is whether a space works — circulation, service logic, staffing, yield per ping. Narrative is whether a space is remembered — material, light, atmosphere, the thing still on you after you walk out.

We put both words into the name of the practice, not because they sound complete together, but because we once delivered only half of it, and paid the tuition.

1. The seduction of narrative

Early on there was a project where nearly all our attention went into a single U-shaped bar.

On the drawing it held together beautifully: a complete ring, the bartender at its centre, guests watching from three sides. Material, light, proportion — every decision pointed at the same thing, making the bar the narrative core of the room. We photographed it before opening. The images were good. The client was happy. So were we.

Then it opened.

Within two weeks everything surfaced. That complete ring had left the floor staff no way through. A server had to walk the entire length of the bar to reach the other side, and at peak hours people jammed at the same single gap. The pass had been placed by symmetry rather than by the sequence of plating, so every dish travelled further than it needed to. Delay accumulated into broken rhythm, and the floor team started to come apart — not because they lacked skill, but because the space would not let them use it.

That is the seduction of narrative: it pays out on the drawing. Beauty can be signed off immediately; you know on the screen that you got it right. Operation cannot. It answers only once people are working inside and the seats are full — and by then the walls are up.

"Beautiful closes before opening night. Usable only begins to be tested after."

2. The weight of operation

When we redesigned it, we changed neither the materials nor the lighting. What we changed was the order of the decisions.

Circulation first, form second. The ring was cut open. The missing arc is a loss in a photograph and a lifeline in service: the floor team now had a path of its own and no longer had to fight the kitchen for the same gap.

The pass was placed by the plating sequence. How many hands a dish passes through between kitchen and table, how many steps it travels, whether it needs a surface to rest on — that path gets drawn first, and the shape of the bar grows around it. Reverse the order and you get a room that is lovely and costs two hundred extra steps a day.

Seat count was derived backwards from staffing. Not fill the seats and then see whether the team copes, but ask first how many tables this headcount can genuinely serve, and place seats accordingly. Those extra covers read as revenue on paper. In practice they lower the quality of every table in the room.

None of these decisions appears in a photograph. All of them decide whether the space is still there in its third year.

The project was later included in the Michelin Guide. We would not call that a design achievement — that belongs to the team running it every day. But at least the space was not in the way. This is de nuit, the restaurant Mo has held a stake in and helped operate since 2019. Owner-first spatial logic is not a stance. It is something the invoices, the turnover rate and the peak-hour floor force on you.

3. Why both have to be there

A space with only narrative is a stage set. It works extremely well for the first three months — it photographs, it circulates, people will come once. From the fourth month the people who came have no reason to return, and the team inside has already been worn down.

A space with only operation is a factory. Circulation flows, yield is healthy, every link makes sense — and there is no reason to remember it. Guests will not dislike it. They also will not think of it. In a market with too many choices, not being thought of is the same as not existing.

For a space to survive, both have to hold.

Monte is thirteen ping, and the first decision was where the kitchen went — that is operation. But once that was fixed, the curvature of the bar was derived from the social distance between strangers sitting side by side — that is narrative. Both happened on the same plan, not in sequence but at once.

The white facade of Rakutaru Robatayaki looks like a purely narrative decision: on an old street full of roller shutters and cabling, white is a sense of boundary. But that white wall also solved something entirely practical. Guests of a robatayaki need to know what kind of place this is while still on the pavement, not after they have walked in. The facade is the brand's first sentence and a filter on who walks through the door.

Good decisions rarely do only one thing. Put operation and narrative on the same table and they start solving each other's problems. Handle them separately and they start costing each other.

4. How this shows up in our process

If both have to hold, the process cannot be "design it, then check whether it works."

So in our five-stage process, brand understanding and spatial strategy are two separate stages, and both sit before design development. Brand understanding is where narrative comes from: what this brand believes, what it wants to be remembered for. Spatial strategy is the skeleton of operation: who works inside, how a day is paced, when it gets hardest. Only once both answers exist do we begin developing the design.

Construction supervision stays inside the process rather than being handed off, for the same reason. A circulation route that works on paper fails the moment a joiner builds a unit five centimetres deeper than drawn — and nobody will tell you about those five centimetres unless someone is on site watching.

We are not the only practice that cares whether a space works. We are one of the few that wrote it into the name — and once it is in the name, delivering half is not available to us.
